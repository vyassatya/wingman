FROM A BAKING INTEREST TO A LISTED COMPANY

Origin and growth

FROM A BAKING INTEREST TO A LISTED COMPANY

Gulapat 'May' Kanokwatanawan developed an interest in baking while young and published the May Made dessert cookbook series after university. She founded the business with Maetup 'Ming' Suwan. The company began in 2005 as Sea Munch Co., before shifting to the After You dessert-cafe format in 2007.

Year Milestone Business significance
2005 Sea Munch Co. established with registered capital equivalent to about ₹57.5 lakh. The business began modestly rather than as a large chain.
2007 After You dessert cafe launched with Shibuya Honey Toast as its defining product. One recognisable product gave customers a specific reason to visit.
2011 Take-home cookies and packaged desserts added. Revenue was extended beyond dine-in consumption.
2015-16 Central factory established; After You listed on Thailand's MAI market in 2016. Production infrastructure supported consistency and expansion.
2024-25 Butter Bun entered 7-Eleven, followed by ready-to-drink After You tea. A cafe brand began reaching customers through everyday retail.
2026 Investor disclosures list 61 After You branches. The flagship has become a sizeable domestic network.

Reported facts: After You 2025 One Report and investor disclosures. INR equivalents use a reference exchange rate of ₹2.875 per unit of Thai currency on 7 September 2026. The 2005 figure is a current INR conversion, not inflation-adjusted value.

HOW ONE PRODUCT BECAME A PLATFORM

The growth mechanism

Growth layer What After You did Why it mattered
Clear memory Made Shibuya Honey Toast the brand's signature. Customers could associate the business with a specific experience.
Product theatre Used warm toast, melting ice cream, tall shaved ice and molten centres. Texture and presentation made the visit easy to share and discuss.
Format expansion Added kakigori, warm desserts, beverages and take-home products. The menu broadened without losing the original product identity.
Operational control Invested in factories, distribution and employee training. Standardised inputs helped maintain consistency across branches.
Visible locations Expanded through premium malls and high-footfall destinations. Queues became public evidence of demand and reinforced awareness.
Retail reach Moved buns and beverages into convenience stores. Purchase occasions extended beyond a planned cafe visit.

WHAT INDIAN HOME BAKERS CAN LEARN

Broad case-study lessons

The relevant lesson is not that every baker needs a cafe or a version of honey toast. It is that a small food business becomes easier to remember and scale when product identity, repeatability and distribution develop in the right order.

Learning What it means for an emerging bakery
Earn recall before variety One product should explain why the brand deserves attention. A large menu can dilute that memory.
Build variations around a stable base New flavours can create novelty while ingredients, process and training remain manageable.
Design for conversation Taste must lead, but texture, serving ritual and presentation increase organic discovery.
Separate celebration from frequency Large cakes bring occasion-led revenue; smaller everyday desserts create more reasons to return.
Document the process early Recipe cards, portion control and preparation standards make quality less dependent on the founder.
Let demand lead expansion Production capacity, outlets and retail distribution should follow proven products rather than precede them.

Context, not a demand forecast

India's food-service market is large and the organised segment is expected to grow faster than the overall sector. However, the broad bakery estimate includes bread, biscuits and other mass categories. It should not be treated as the addressable market for premium home-baked desserts. For a small bakery, local repeat orders and the share of sales coming from its defining product are more useful signals than national market size.

THE CASE STUDY IN ONE LINE

Conclusion

After You shows how a distinctive hero product can create the first wave of demand, but lasting scale comes from turning that product recognition into a repeatable operating system and progressively wider distribution.
