Executive Summary

The Opportunity in 5 Points

  • Revenue per listing has roughly doubled while supply has been cut in half. Active short-term rental listings fell 55.2% year-on-year to 11,501, while average annual revenue per listing rose 112.4% to $27.8K (AirDNA, trailing twelve months to July 2026). This is enforcement rationing supply, not organic demand growth.

  • The real estate market has stalled, not corrected. Condo prices are flat at roughly THB 150,000/sqm (+0.6% year-on-year, Colliers Thailand), while villa take-up fell from 50.2% to a forecast 45.1% for 2026. Buyers have some leverage, but there is no distressed-asset window.

  • Almost no legal path exists for condo-based daily rentals. Any stay under 30 days is a hotel business under the Hotel Act B.E. 2547; condominiums cannot realistically obtain a hotel licence, so daily or weekly condo rentals are unlawful regardless of platform listing status.

  • Villas hold the only workable legal structure for short-term income. Properties with 8 rooms or fewer and 30 guests or fewer can register a 5-year Certificate of Exemption. This is the path AirDNA's Regulation subscore (72/100) is implicitly pricing in for the Phuket market.

  • Foreign ownership caps the ceiling on scale. Condo freehold is capped at 49% of a building's floor area; land ownership is closed to foreigners outright, so villas require a 30year leasehold. Any operating model has to be built around leasehold economics from the first unit.

What This Means for Phuket

A 55% collapse in listing count alongside a doubling of revenue per listing is not what organic demand growth looks like — it is what a market looks like after non-compliant supply gets removed and remaining demand concentrates on fewer, apparently compliant properties. Section 2 sets out why that removal happened and what it means for any new entrant.

2. The Regulatory Reality

Thailand's Hotel Act B.E. 2547 (2004) is the single governing law, and it has not been amended for platforms like Airbnb. Any paid stay under 30 days is legally a hotel business, full stop — this applies to a standalone villa exactly as it does to a condominium unit.

Structure Legal Status Requirement
Condo, stay under 30 days Illegal without a hotel licence (rarely obtainable for condos) Hotel Act B.E. 2547, Sections 15 & 59
Condo, stay 30 days or more Legal Standard residential lease; no licence needed
Villa/house, ≤8 rooms & ≤30 guests Legal with registration 5-year Certificate of Exemption at the local district office
Villa/house, larger than above Illegal without a full hotel licence Hotel Act B.E. 2547
Any structure, foreign guests Separate obligation regardless of the above TM30 filing; fine of THB 800–2,000 per unreported guest
Penalties

  • Operating without a licence or registered exemption: up to 1 year imprisonment, an initial fine of THB 20,000, plus THB 10,000 for every additional day the violation continues.

  • A Thai court judgment in early 2026 confirmed that only monthly-or-longer rentals are exempt from the Hotel Act, closing off any ambiguity that a well-run "condotel" model might survive.

Enforcement in 2025–2026

Enforcement in Phuket is run jointly with the Immigration Bureau, alongside the Department of Provincial Administration and Revenue Department. Authorities now cross-reference tax filings, TM30 guest records, and booking-platform data rather than relying on complaints — a shift from a complaint-driven to a data-driven enforcement model. Platforms themselves (Airbnb, Booking.com) do not verify licence status independently; they list first and remove only when notified of a specific violation.

What This Means for a Phuket Airbnb Business

A model built on daily-rental condo income is not compliant, whatever the platform allows a host to list. A model built on villa ownership within the exemption threshold, or on 30-day-plus stays, is compliant — and is functionally the market that AirDNA's Regulation subscore of 72/100 is describing.

The Phuket Property Visa

A THB 3 million freehold condo purchase, a THB 85,000/month lease, or a 3-year lease worth approximately THB 3.06 million qualifies for a renewable 1-year long-stay visa, piloted in Phuket since October 2025. Applicants must report to immigration every 90 days, and the visa is revocable for illegal activity.

Uptake has been narrow: 25–35 approvals in Phuket by February 2026, out of 650+ national applications. Local tourism operators have publicly opposed the THB 3 million threshold as too low, warning it could be used to acquire multiple units for short-term letting. Treat this as a residency tool for an owner-operator today, not yet a scaled acquisition channel.

5. Unit Economics (Illustrative)

Two structures represent the realistic choices for a Phuket operator: a villa run under the exemption route, and a condo restricted to 30-day-plus leasing. Figures below are market-level benchmarks, not a specific-property underwrite.

Interpretation

The villa/STR model carries the higher headline yield but also the only real compliance burden and a management-fee drag of 20–30% off the top. The condo/long-let model is simpler and fully legal but yields roughly half. There is currently no product offering both condo-level convenience and villa-level STR income within the legal framework.

Closing Note

Phuket's short-term rental data is telling an enforcement story, not a demand story. Fewer listings earning far more each is what a market looks like after non-compliant supply gets removed — not what organic growth looks like. Entering now, on the compliant side of that line, is timed better than entering was two years ago, when daily condo rentals were still tolerated in practice.

The only route to a multi-unit STR portfolio that survives 2026-level enforcement is villas under the exemption threshold, acquired on 30-year leasehold, run at the 7.8–8.4% net yields the better-managed portfolios are already achieving. A condo-heavy strategy caps out at long-termlease economics (4–6% gross) unless paired with a licensed hotel or branded-residence structure.

Underwrite two or three villas in an established west-coast corridor before condo units, and build compliance registration — exemption certificate, TM30 — into the acquisition cost from the first unit, not as a retrofit.
