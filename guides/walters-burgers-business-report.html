Walters Burgers

Business Research Report: The gap Walters fills, gourmet burgers at a mid-range price

November 2026 | Based on stock exchange filings, earnings calls, news reports and listing data. Estimates are marked.

1. The Short Version

  • Mumbai's burger market has an empty middle. Mass chains sell cheap burgers. Gourmet brands like Good Flippin' Burgers charge about Rs 1,200 for two. Very few brands sell a gourmet burger for much less than that.

  • Walters sits right in that middle. It sells smashed lamb, truffle mushroom and katsu chicken burgers on house-made potato buns at about Rs 600 for two, roughly half what gourmet rivals charge.

  • The trick is size, not shortcuts. Walters makes smaller, one-handed burgers and sells them in combo boxes. Smaller portions keep the price down without cheaper ingredients.

  • A listed parent keeps costs low. Walters is owned by Speciality Restaurants Limited, the Mainland China company. It shares the parent's commissary, cloud kitchens and delivery know-how, and needs no outside money.

  • The gap is proven on taste, not yet on scale. Its main kitchen holds a 4.4 Zomato rating from over 1,400 ratings. But it has about 7 outlets against a plan of about 20 by March 2027, and has never shared store sales.

2. The Gap: Mumbai's Burger Price Ladder

India's quick service restaurant market is worth about $27.8 billion in 2025 and is expected to reach about $43.5 billion by 2030, growing about 9.4% a year (Mordor Intelligence). In Mumbai's burger market, brands bunch at two ends of the price ladder.

Price band (for two) Who serves it What the customer gets Crowding
Under Rs 500 McDonald's, Burger King, Burger Singh (200+ outlets), Jumboking Fast, cheap, frozen patties, Indian flavours Very crowded
Rs 500 to Rs 800 Walters, a few single-outlet local spots Gourmet fillings in a smaller, neater burger Thin
Rs 1,000 to Rs 1,500 Good Flippin' Burgers (67 outlets, about Rs 1,200 for two) Big gourmet burgers, sit-down feel Crowded and growing
Rs 2,000 and up Chef-led spots like Veronica's (Hunger Inc) Restaurant-grade burgers, queues Small, niche

The middle band is where a regular office worker or student eats on a weekday. They want better than a mass-chain burger but will not spend Rs 600 a head on a weekday lunch. That buyer is under-served, and that is the space Walters is built for.

3. How Walters Fills the Gap

What makes it gourmet What keeps the price mid-range
Fillings: OG Smashed Lamb with two patties, Truffle Shroomson, Chicken Katsu Sando, Walter O Fish Smaller, one-handed burgers. Less meat and bread per burger without cheaper ingredients
House-made yellow potato buns, designed so the burger holds together in one hand Combo boxes like the Quad (four slider-size burgers) give variety at one set price
Sides like truffle fries and sriracha chicken popcorn, plus shakes Small stores and food courts, not big high-street dining rooms
Packaging built for delivery, so burgers arrive neat Central commissary cooks for many stores at once

Founder Avik Chatterjee calls it fixing the big, messy burger. The brand is named after Walter, credited with inventing the one-handed burger in America in 1921. Reviewers keep returning to the same point: the burgers are compact, full of flavour and easy to eat. That is the mid-range promise in one line.

4. Why Rivals Cannot Easily Move Into the Middle

Rival type Why the middle is hard for them
Mass chains Kitchens and supply chains are built for low-cost frozen patties. Gourmet fillings would break their cost model and speed
Gourmet chains like Good Flippin' Brand is built on big burgers. Cutting price means cutting size, which weakens what customers pay them for
Chef-led restaurants Rent, chefs and dine-in service cost too much to sell at Rs 300 a head
New independent brands Can make the product, but lack a commissary, cloud kitchens and cash to open many stores

5. Why Walters Can Hold the Mid-Range Price

The gap only works if costs stay low, and this is where the parent matters. Speciality Restaurants made Rs 476 crore in revenue in FY26 with a Rs 20.7 crore profit, and runs 125 outlets in 11 cities. Walters uses what the group already has.

Parent advantage How it helps the price
Maharashtra commissary Built to supply 20 to 25 Walters stores, so each store needs a smaller kitchen and staff
11 cloud kitchens and a 32% delivery share Walters can reach delivery buyers without paying for dine-in space
Light store build FY27 capex of about Rs 5 crore across about 25 Walters and Sweet Bengal stores, roughly Rs 20 lakh each
Own cash Rs 162 crore in cash and investments; no investors pushing for faster or pricier growth

The founder fits this model. Avik is the son of Anjan Chatterjee, who started Mainland China in 1995 and took Speciality public in 2012. Avik studied at Kingston University, ran a midnight delivery start-up at 18, and joined the group in 2015 to build new formats, including Hoppipola. He became CEO on 1 June 2026, so the brand's founder now controls the group's capital.

6. Can the Gap Pay?

Walters does not publish store numbers. These are analyst estimates built from the Rs 600 price for two and normal Mumbai quick service costs.

Line (per outlet, estimated) Share of sales What drives it
Food and packaging 32% to 34% Premium fillings, house-made buns, delivery boxes
Staff, rent and utilities 28% to 31% Small counter teams and small units
Delivery app commission 10% to 12% About 25% commission on a large delivery share
Marketing About 3% Bloggers, app listings and discounts
Outlet profit before overheads About 18% to 20% What is left from each outlet

A busy outlet serving about 150 walk-in guests and 120 delivery orders a day would bring in about Rs 2.7 crore a year. At a build cost of Rs 30 lakh to Rs 50 lakh, a good store can pay for itself in under a year. At 20 stores, Walters would add Rs 30 crore to Rs 60 crore a year, or 5% to 10% of the parent's Rs 600 crore FY27 revenue target.

What the company has said about growth: about 15 new Walters stores in FY27, all in Mumbai and company-owned, a new cloud kitchen, and new desserts and drinks. If Walters reaches 20 stores with steady ratings, it will have shown the mid-range gourmet gap is real and big enough to take to Pune and beyond.
